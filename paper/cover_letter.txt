Cover letter for Measurement Science and Technology
15 September 2026

To the Editor,
Measurement Science and Technology, IOP Publishing

Dear Editor,

We submit for your consideration the Paper "How many views does a path-length
measurement need? Trading camera views for time instants under a fixed frame
budget".

A camera network that reports how far a target moved must spend a finite
processing budget, and that budget can buy either more simultaneous views at
each time instant or more instants. The two choices act on the two opposite
biases of the same estimate. The bias trade-off itself is established in the
telemetry literature, where a single sensor has a fixed sampling rate and the
only practical response is post-hoc correction; the allocation question that a
multi-sensor instrument faces is not addressed there. We measure that error
surface directly.

We believe the work fits the scope of Measurement Science and Technology for
three reasons. First, the contribution is an advance in measurement technique
rather than the application of an established one: we identify a design
variable that the existing treatment of this bias does not contain, and we
give a quantitative rule for setting it. Second, the article contains a full
consideration of measurement uncertainty, as the journal requires: we give a
top-down budget in the vocabulary of the GUM and the VIM, with a determined
and separately reported sampling bias of -8.2 mm and an expanded uncertainty
of 14.9 mm at a coverage factor of two, and we use the reconstruction error as
a consistency check on that budget rather than as an additional term. Third,
the central negative result is a measurement-design guideline: we bound the
achievable gain from non-uniform temporal placement at 8 to 25 per cent even
when the reference trajectory is used to choose the placement, which tells a
practitioner not to invest there.

Two features of the study may be of particular interest to your reviewers. The
confirmation experiments were pre-registered: the protocol, the candidate
list, the decision gates and the source-file hashes were frozen before the
confirmation records were read, and every prediction was sealed by a
cryptographic hash before the reference was loaded. And we report a
methodological caution that applies well beyond this measurand: what appeared
in our data to be the classical noise-accumulation limb is in fact exposure to
rare gross outliers, visible in the mean but not in the median or the
ninetieth percentile, and the two mechanisms call for different engineering
responses.

We state plainly in the manuscript what we do not claim. The bias mechanism
belongs to the telemetry literature we cite, and the existence of saturation
in camera count has been reported in biomechanics; our contribution is the
budget axis that couples them, the mechanism behind the saturation, and the
two bounds. The saturation point of three views is a property of the
instruments we measured, not a universal constant, and the manuscript gives
the reason it cannot be universal.

The work is original, has not been published elsewhere and is not under
consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the submission and
declare no conflict of interest. The use of an artificial intelligence
assistant in preparing the analysis software and the manuscript is disclosed
in the acknowledgments.

We would be grateful for your consideration.
