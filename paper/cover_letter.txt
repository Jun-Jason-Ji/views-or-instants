Cover letter for Measurement Science and Technology
16 September 2026

To the Editor,
Measurement Science and Technology, IOP Publishing

Dear Editor,

We submit for your consideration the Paper "Allocating camera views and time instants for trajectory-length
measurement under a frame-processing budget".

We study how a multi-camera trajectory-length measurement divides a finite
processing budget between simultaneous views and sampling instants. The
contribution is an explicit evaluation of the joint allocation surface,
including accuracy, completion and reference sensitivity, for a specified
measurement chain. The familiar sparse-versus-dense sampling bias is credited
to the telemetry literature; we examine the additional budget constraint
introduced by multiple cameras.

At exactly 84 frames per window, a calibration-selected three-view
configuration gives mean absolute error 9.60 mm, compared with 50.77 mm for
seven views on 60 windows. This contrast persists under three noise settings,
reference decimation and deletion of each record. A broader exploratory grid
of two to seven views and four budget caps nevertheless reverses the ranking
at 168 frames, where rare extreme errors affect the triple. The article reports
this counterexample and does not recommend a universal view count. Original
frozen candidate decisions are presented separately from the exploratory grid;
the records do not constitute a newly collected independent validation set.

The article also includes disjoint-camera comparisons, independent-pixel-noise
controls with shared cameras, temporal error diagnostics and a fixed robust
triangulation baseline. These qualify possible explanations rather than claim
an identified common-mode mechanism. A reference-assisted curvature sampling
rule is evaluated on explicit common-success populations, with its distinct
eligibility policy explained; it is not a general optimization bound. Error
reductions under a combined robustness gate are reported together with the
loss of completed windows. In a secondary single-camera experiment, the
pre-specified primary criterion is not fully met; that result is reported
alongside the successful secondary analysis.

Measurement uncertainty is discussed through observed error dispersion,
uncorrected bias, reference-resolution sensitivity, and the calibration and
reference contributions that remain unquantified. We do not present the
descriptive error scale as a validated coverage interval. Protocol freezing,
prior quality-control exposure of the primary confirmation records, prediction
sealing, and subsequent exploratory analyses are distinguished explicitly.

We believe the work is relevant to readers designing vision-based measurement
systems under limited processing resources. Its conclusions concern the
tested processing chain, rather than camera acquisition costs or a universal
number of cameras.

The work is original, has not been published elsewhere and is not under
consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the submission and
declare no conflict of interest. The use of an artificial intelligence
assistant in preparing the analysis software and the manuscript is disclosed
in the acknowledgments.

We would be grateful for your consideration.
