We evaluate processing-budget allocation between camera views and sampling instants for trajectory-length measurement. A calibration-based subset rule is tested across two to seven views and four budget caps, with explicit frame use, completion and tail errors. The three-view advantage at 84 frames persists under noise-parameter, reference-resolution and record-deletion checks, but reverses at 168 frames because of rare large errors. The contribution is a reproducible evaluation procedure and evidence of budget-dependent trade-offs, rather than a universal camera-count prescription.
