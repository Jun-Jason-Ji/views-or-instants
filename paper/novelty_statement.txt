The bias trade-off between sparse and dense temporal sampling of a trajectory
is established for single-sensor telemetry, where the sampling rate is fixed
by the device. We show that a camera network has a second lever competing for
the same processing budget, and measure the resulting error surface: accuracy
saturates at three views, so the budget should buy time instants, giving the
same measurement at 43% of the frames. We explain the saturation, which the
literature reports but does not account for: the reconstruction error is
largely common-mode, and a path length is invariant to a common translation.
